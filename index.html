The idea

Same purchase. Two days apart. Twenty-eight days of difference.

Float is the stretch between the day you buy something and the day the statement carrying it comes due — the time you legitimately hold your own money before the issuer wants it back. Buy just before a statement closes and you get the short end. Buy the day after, and the charge falls onto next month's statement instead.

27
Days of float buying the day before the statement closes.
55
Days of float buying the day after — the same purchase, same card.
+28
Days you keep the money, for waiting forty-eight hours.

Worked on a card that closes on the 28th and is due 25 days later. Your own dates give your own numbers — which is exactly why the app does the counting instead of teaching you a rule of thumb.

How it works

Three facts per card. That is the whole setup.

No bank connection, no account to create, no permission to grant. You read two dates off a statement you already have.

Perks & benefits

Float tells you when. Perks tell you which.

Rental car coverage, no foreign transaction fee, cell phone protection, a lounge you forgot you had — you are paying for these whether you use them or not, and they are scattered across four different issuer websites. The Benefits screen turns the wallet inside out: the benefit is the heading, and your own cards are the answer underneath it.

Four honest answers, not two

A benefit is not a yes-or-no per card. It is four different sentences, and three of them only mean anything if the app says them out loud.

No fee

You confirmed a zero on that card. Safe, and we stand behind it.

Charges a fee

You confirmed a number, and the number is shown with it. No rounding, no "some cards may".

Not published

The issuer page we cite says nothing about this one. That is not the same as free — and this screen refuses to let silence read as good news.

Not connected

The card is not linked to a product yet, so we know nothing either way. Pick its product and it moves out of here.

Around 280 card products ship inside the app, each carrying the issuer page it was read from and the date it was read. When something goes stale, the app tells you it is stale instead of guessing.

Privacy

No accounts. No tracking. Nothing about you ever reaches us.

This is not a posture, it is the architecture. There is no server to hold your data, so there is nothing to breach, subpoena, sell, or quietly change our minds about in two years.

No card numbers, ever

There is no field for a full card number, a CVV, or a PIN — not hidden, not optional, not encrypted somewhere. Last four digits only, so you can tell your own cards apart.

No bank connection

No login, no aggregator, no read-only link. The app cannot see your transactions because it was never given a way to.

No balances

No screen shows what you owe and no field stores it. The app does calendar arithmetic; it has no business knowing your balance and does not ask.

Your iCloud, not our cloud

If you want your cards on two devices, they travel through your own iCloud account under your own Apple Account. We are not a party to it and cannot read it.

One network call, and you can see it

The card catalog checks for updated card facts — a signed, read-only file, the same for everybody. It sends nothing about you, and you can switch it off.

Support goes through your own Mail app

Tap Contact support and the app hands a written message to Mail. You press send. Nothing is transmitted by us, and there is no help-desk vendor in between.

Do I need to connect my bank?

No. You type two dates off a statement you already have. The app has no login screen for a bank and no way to reach one.

Is this a way to borrow money cheaply?

The opposite. Float only exists while you pay your statement in full and on time — carry a balance and interest starts from the purchase date, and the whole idea evaporates.

This app is for people who already pay in full and would rather do it on the latest date the issuer allows.

Does it tell me what to buy?

Never. It has no opinion about your spending and no idea what you spend. You decide to buy something; the app tells you which card gives you the most days on it, and when.

Where do the perks come from?

From the issuer's own published page for that card product, read and recorded with the date it was read. Around 280 products ship with the app, so most cards fill themselves in the moment you pick them.

If the issuer never published something, the app says not published rather than guessing. And anything you confirm yourself always wins over the catalog.

What if my issuer changes a date?

Change it in the app and every number updates. Because there is no bank connection, the app cannot find out on its own — which is why the statement is always the authority, and the app says so.
