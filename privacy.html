Privacy Policy

Last updated: [effective date] · Applies to the SwipeThisCard app and this website.

This policy explains what the SwipeThisCard app and swipethiscard.com handle, where it goes, and what you can do about it. “We” and “us” means Tessvane LLC, the company that makes SwipeThisCard. This policy is part of our Terms of Service.

1. The short version

  • No account. There is nothing to sign up for. We don’t know who you are unless you email us.
  • What you type stays yours. It lives on your device and, when you’re signed in to iCloud, in your own private iCloud account. We run no server that stores it, and we cannot see it.
  • No tracking. No analytics, no advertising and no third-party code in the app. No cookies on this website.
  • Nothing sold, nothing shared. We never sell or share information about you.
  • One thing goes out on its own. At most once a day, the app checks a public file of card facts. It sends nothing you typed, and you can switch it off.

2. What you type into the app

SwipeThisCard knows only what you type into it:

  • The last four digits of each card, so you can tell your cards apart.
  • The card’s issuer and product, and whether it is a personal card or belongs to a company you name.
  • The day each statement closes and each payment comes due, whether the card is on auto-pay, and whether you’re paying interest on it.
  • Facts about the card you choose to record, such as its rewards and bonus categories, annual fee, foreign transaction fee, credit limit, expiration and any promotional end date.
  • Whether the card is with you and set up for tap to pay.
  • Each statement’s balance, only if you choose to type it in, dated to that statement, and whether you’ve marked it paid.
  • Your settings, such as reminders, widgets and appearance, and any note you add to a reminder.

The app never asks for a full card number, a security code, a PIN, a bank login or your current balance, and there is nowhere in it to type one. It has no connection to your bank or your card issuer.

3. Where it lives

On your device, in the app’s own storage, protected by your device’s passcode and encryption. When you’re signed in to iCloud, the app also keeps a copy in the private database of your own iCloud account, so your cards appear on your other devices. Apple holds that copy under your Apple Account and Apple’s own privacy policy; we have no access to it. To keep your cards on one device only, turn SwipeThisCard off in your device’s iCloud settings.

The app’s widgets, reminders and Siri answers read the same information on your device. They don’t send it anywhere.

4. What leaves your device, and when

Only these, and nothing else:

  • The card-facts check. At most once a day, and only while the app is open, SwipeThisCard checks a public file of card facts — published rates, fees, rewards and benefits — at swipethiscard.app/catalog/v1/manifest.json, and downloads the catalog beside it only when there is a newer one. The request carries nothing you typed and nothing of ours that could identify you: no account, no device ID, no cookie. Like any web request, it shows the host your internet (IP) address and the details your device adds to every request on its own: the app’s name and build, the system’s version, and your preferred languages. The files are signed, so the app ignores anything we didn’t publish, and you can open both in any browser. Switch the check off in the app under Settings › Privacy and data › Keep card facts up to date; everything else works offline.
  • iCloud, when you’re signed in: between your devices and your own iCloud account, through Apple.
  • Purchases, through Apple (section 6).
  • An email to us, only if you send one (section 8).
  • An export, only where you send it (section 7).
  • A web page you choose to open, such as an issuer’s page from a card’s details. That site sees your visit under its own privacy policy. The app adds nothing about you to the link.
  • A calendar event you add. Adding a due date to your calendar saves the card’s name, its last four digits, the date and your note in your own calendar, which syncs wherever that calendar does, such as iCloud, Google or Exchange.
  • Siri, Shortcuts and Control Center, when you use them: Apple handles the request under its own privacy policy, and the app answers from what is on your device.

5. Reminders, widgets, Siri and Face ID

Reminders are scheduled by iOS on your device; no server of ours sends them. Reminders, widgets and Siri can show or say a card’s name, its last four digits and a company name you gave it — never an amount — where others might see or hear them, such as on your Lock Screen. You decide how much they show in the app under Settings › Widgets, and in your device’s own notification settings.

Face ID Lock is off unless you turn it on. When it’s on, iOS checks your face, fingerprint or passcode and tells the app only whether it matched; the app never receives your face or fingerprint data. The lock covers the app and Siri’s answers, but not widgets or reminders, which follow the switches above.

6. Purchases

Apple sells and bills Unlimited Cards. We never see your name, your Apple Account, your payment details or your address. The app asks Apple, on your device, whether you have an active purchase, and keeps the answer on your device. Apple gives us sales and subscription reports that do not identify you. Apple’s privacy policy covers what Apple does with your purchase.

7. Your export

The app can save your cards as a CSV file: dates, statuses and card facts, including credit limits and company names, but never card numbers, security codes or balances. Once you save or share it, it’s your file, and wherever you put it, that place’s terms apply. Keep it somewhere safe.

8. When you contact us

Contact Support in the app writes your message and hands it to your own mail app; nothing is sent until you send it yourself. It adds a short technical note you can read, change or delete first: app version, iOS version, device model, number of cards, your plan, and the screen you came from. It never adds your card details. If your device has no mail account set up, the app copies the message for you to paste into another mail app.

Email to hello@swipethiscard.com is kept in our mailbox, hosted by Google Workspace. We use it only to answer you and to fix what you report.

9. This website

swipethiscard.com is a set of plain pages hosted by Vercel. It sets no cookies, runs no analytics, shows no ads, and loads nothing from any other company. Like any web host, Vercel keeps short-lived server logs — internet address, browser type, page requested — for security and reliability. We don’t use them to identify anyone.

The buttons to join the beta or to hear when the app launches open an email in your own mail app. If you send it, we keep your address on a list of our own and use it only to invite you to the beta or to tell you when the app ships. No mailing service and no tracking pixels. If that ever changes, this policy changes first.

10. If you test a beta

Beta versions come through Apple’s TestFlight. Apple shares with us the name and email address you were invited under, when you installed each build and how often you’ve opened it, and, if you choose to send them, your feedback, screenshots and crash reports with your device details.

We also keep a short list of testers: your first name, country, email, roughly how many cards you carry, whether you have business cards, the devices you test on, the dates we invited you and heard from you, and the offer code for your free year. If you choose to tell us you are colorblind, we note that too, because the app is built for colorblind cardholders and we want it tested by them. We delete the list once every tester’s free year has been issued and the beta is over, keeping only the emails of testers who asked to hear about launches.

11. Crash reports Apple shares with us

If you’ve chosen in your device’s settings to share analytics with app developers, Apple may send us crash reports and summary usage figures. They don’t identify you to us. You can change that in Settings › Privacy & Security › Analytics & Improvements. The app itself contains no crash reporter and no analytics code.

12. Who else is involved

Four companies play a part, each under its own privacy terms and only for the job named:

  • Apple — the App Store, purchases, iCloud, TestFlight, Siri, reminders and Face ID.
  • Cloudflare — serves the public card-facts files.
  • Vercel — hosts this website.
  • Google Workspace — hosts our email.

13. What we never do

We never sell or share information about you, show you ads, or track you across other companies’ apps or websites; the app never asks for permission to track you. We don’t buy information about you, combine what we hold with other sources, or use it to profile you or make automated decisions about you. And the app will never start sending anything new about you without saying so in the app first.

14. How long things are kept

  • What you typed in the app. We never hold it. It stays until you delete it: a card with its history, from inside the app; everything on a device, by deleting the app; and the iCloud copy, from your device’s iCloud storage settings.
  • Email. As long as we need it to help you and keep a record of what we told you. Ask and we delete it, unless the law or a live dispute requires us to keep it.
  • The launch list. Until you ask to leave it.
  • The beta list. As section 10 says.
  • Host logs. Kept by Vercel and Cloudflare for their own short periods. We don’t copy them.

15. Your choices and rights

Wherever you live, you can ask us what we hold about you, to correct it, to delete it, or for a copy. For most cardholders the answer is nothing: what you typed in the app is already in your hands, and the export gives you a copy. If you have emailed us, or joined the beta or the launch list, write to hello@swipethiscard.com from the same address and we will act within 30 days. Someone else may ask for you with your written permission. We will never treat you differently for asking.

We don’t sell or share personal information, so there is nothing to opt out of. This website tracks no one, so it treats every visit the same, with or without a Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control signal. If you are in the European Union or the United Kingdom, we rely on your consent for the launch and beta lists (you can withdraw it at any time), and on our legitimate interest in answering you and keeping this site secure; you may also complain to your local data protection authority.

16. Security

Your card information stays behind your own passcode, Face ID and Apple Account. Because we never hold it, a breach on our side cannot expose it. The card-facts files are signed, so the app ignores any file we did not publish. The little we do hold — email and the lists above — sits in accounts only we can reach. No system is perfect: if a breach ever affects information we hold, we will tell you as the law requires.

17. Children

SwipeThisCard is for adults 18 and older and is not directed at children. If we learn that we hold a child’s information, such as an email, we delete it.

18. Where information is handled

We are a US company. Our email and this website are run from the United States, and the card-facts files are served from Cloudflare’s servers around the world. If you write to us from outside the US, your email is handled in the US.

19. Changes to this policy

The date at the top of this page shows the current version. If a change matters — to what leaves your device, what we hold, or your rights — we’ll say so in the app before it takes effect.
